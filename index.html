About

I'm an iOS engineer who cares about the details: smooth animations, fast launches, and code a teammate can read a year later.

At FanDuel I work on the Sportsbook team, where my job is making one of the busiest sports betting apps in the country launch fast, scroll smoothly, and stay up when millions of people open it at once on Super Bowl Sunday. Before that I spent three and a half years on the Daily Fantasy app, shipping features and leading its rebrand, and interned on the iOS teams at Twitter and Cabana Health.

AI is the other half of how I work, and I use it the way a lead uses a strong team. I write the spec, direct the agents against it, and own the architecture and every review, so the speed comes without giving up judgment. That runs through every stage of the job: turning ideas into written contracts before a line is written, generating and reviewing code, and automating CI and ticket workflows. I keep up with new models, agent frameworks, and tooling as they land, and I'm constantly testing what they can do so I know exactly where to trust them and where not to.

I live in New York. Outside of work I run, read, and chase whatever I'm learning next, and I get outside whenever I can: golf when there's time, and surfing when I can get to a coast. The photo above is my dad and me.

Projects

Divvy

An iOS app that splits a restaurant bill from a photo of the receipt. I designed and built the whole thing: the SwiftUI app, the Python backend, Stripe payments, and the AI layer, where Claude Vision reads the receipt and a natural-language parser lets you say "Sarah had the pasta" and watch the bill assign itself. The parser escalates from a cheap model to a stronger one only when needed, sits behind a provider layer I built to swap between Anthropic, Gemini, and OpenAI, and is scored against an eval corpus on every CI run.

I built it spec-first. Every feature starts with an acceptance contract I write, and I direct AI agents against that contract while owning the architecture, reviewing every change, and holding the line on quality with a full test suite and seventeen written engineering standards. The result is more than 1,500 commits since February, and every one of them went through me.

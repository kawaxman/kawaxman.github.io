About

I'm an iOS engineer who cares about the details: smooth animations, fast launches, and code a teammate can read a year later.

At FanDuel I work on the Sportsbook core app team, where my job is making one of the busiest sports betting apps in the country launch fast, scroll smoothly, and stay up when millions of people open it at once on Super Bowl Sunday. Before that I spent three and a half years on the Daily Fantasy app, shipping features and leading its rebrand, and interned on the iOS teams at Twitter and Cabana Health.

AI is the other half of how I work. I lean on it at every stage of the job: turning ideas into written specs before a line is written, running agent loops that implement and verify against those specs, reviewing the output, and automating CI and ticket workflows. My side projects are built this way end to end. I treat it as a discipline, not a shortcut. I keep up with new models, agent frameworks, and tooling as they land, and I'm constantly testing what they can do so I know exactly where to trust them and where not to.

I live in New York. Outside of work I run, read, and chase whatever I'm learning next, and I get outside whenever I can: golf when there's time, and surfing when I can get to a coast. The photo above is my dad and me.

Projects

Divvy

An iOS app for splitting a restaurant bill from a photo of the receipt. AI runs through the product, not just the build. Claude Vision reads the receipt into line items, and a natural-language parser lets you say "Sarah had the pasta and we split the wine" and have the bill assign itself. A cheap model handles the common case and escalates to a stronger one only when it has to, behind a provider layer that can swap between Anthropic, Gemini, and OpenAI. Parser accuracy is tracked with an eval corpus that runs in CI.

I built it with spec-driven development. Every feature starts as a written acceptance contract of observable behaviors, which becomes the Jira ticket, the test plan, and the definition of done. Claude Code agents then run the delivery loop: plan, have the plan torn apart by an adversarial review, implement, self-verify against the contract on a real simulator with screenshots as evidence, push, and drive a self-fixing PR review bot to green before merging. Every loop has a hard cap, so the agent stops and surfaces state rather than thrashing. Seventeen written AI policy files govern design language, accessibility, security, and quality gates, so the output stays consistent no matter which agent produced it. Since February that process has shipped more than 1,500 commits across 400-plus tickets, while I review the product instead of the diff. Stripe payments, Firebase, SwiftUI, and a full unit and end-to-end test suite.
